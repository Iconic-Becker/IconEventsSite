## Q1. What was different about year two?

User, 2026-10-20, verbatim (covers Q1 to Q4 and facts):

> So in year two, we actually ended up with 1237. So there was a lot that
> changed in the second year. Florida laws changed, so we had to get an ASL
> interpreter request. We ended up finding, this would have completely
> broken the budget and been way too expensive. We ended up finding a
> accommodation by essentially creating a, that cost Danny nothing, by
> taking the live stream and enabling a real-time caption service that the
> attendee was able to tune into from the crowd. The Florida registration
> workshop, so that was new. We had to essentially visqueen the floors,
> which means that plastic completely covered the floor end to end, which
> allowed us to transform the ballrooms into temporary, kind of like medical
> level staging units for tattoo artists. The tropical storm was a really
> big one. That was the hardest thing we got really hit out of nowhere with.
> How we prepared was that about a week out, we started sourcing backup and
> secondary vendors that in the event that shipments were not able to make
> it, we would then go ahead and we would then be able to source locally,
> which is exactly what ended up happening. Several packages got delayed in
> the mail, and we were able to preemptively go to vendors that had already
> been pre-sorted ahead of time to do that. The badges was one of those
> things that didn't arrive, and so we ended up actually finding a local
> vinyl print shop, and we had to use a different and unique kind of
> mechanism for the cardstock, but this still allowed us to print the badges
> on time, get them over to everyone, and still maintain the luxury feel. It
> was still in the Seminole Hard Walk in Hollywood again. I don't remember
> the exact dates. What he wanted from year two that was different from 2023
> was we focused a lot more on having a really eloquent sponsor hall. We
> focused on basically halving the size of the room. And instead of having
> two stages, we had one stage. We had one stage with one major sponsor
> hall, which allowed us to attribute a lot more revenue into the room by
> selling smaller sponsorship packages that were like $5,000 to $6,000 in
> total. Doing so added hundreds of thousands of dollars in sponsorship
> revenue to the event, which allowed us to add diverse opportunities for a
> new expo floor, which the attendees enjoyed a lot more. The sponsors got
> better value out of, and it created a much more dynamic event.

Captured: 1,237 attendees. Seminole Hard Rock, Hollywood ("Hard Walk" is
transcription). Dates not remembered. ASL request met at no cost with
real time captions on the livestream. Visqueen floors for the tattoo
workshops. Storm: backup local vendors sourced a week out; packages
delayed; badges reprinted at a local vinyl print shop. Room halved, one
stage, one major sponsor hall and a new expo floor, with smaller
sponsorship packages. Sponsorship dollar figures are house rule 1: left
off unless Iconic makes an exception.
