# Iconic Events — Brand Voice Guide

*Built from the Iconic Events Brand Guidelines (Edition 02). A working reference for writing in Iconic's voice across proposals, playbooks, client correspondence, decks, and the sales page.*

---

## 1. Who Iconic Is (voice foundation)

Iconic Events is a luxury experiential events agency, founded 2017 on the conviction that the experience industry had drifted toward generic, scalable, and loud. Iconic works in the opposite register: **considered, quiet, specific.** It produces for clients who do not put their names on most of what they own.

The voice flows directly from the positioning. Iconic sells **discretion, command, and inevitability** — so it sounds like it has already handled everything, says only what needs saying, and never seeks approval.

**The one-line personality:**
> The most prepared person in the room, who has done this before, and tells you the next step rather than asking for it.

Think heritage, not hospitality: private libraries, members' rooms, the ritual of a well-set table. Every output should read like it was **engraved, not generated.** Iconic earns that with discipline, not ornament.

## 2. How Iconic Speaks

### Tone dials

| Dimension | Where Iconic sits |
|---|---|
| Formal ←→ Casual | **Composed and precise.** Not stiff, not chatty — the register of a trusted operator. |
| Reserved ←→ Bold | **Reserved but certain.** Quiet on the outside, absolute underneath. |
| Understated ←→ Aspirational | **Understated.** The work speaks; the copy never oversells. |
| Ornate ←→ Direct | **Direct.** Declarative sentences. No padding, no hedging. |
| Vendor ←→ Operator | **Operator.** The voice of someone who runs the room, not one seeking the booking. |

### Core mechanics
- **Declarative, not tentative.** State the plan. "Tasting at 18:00. Three courses, paired." Never "we could do a tasting around six if that works."
- **Recommend, don't offer.** Present *the* route, not a menu. Describe the next step as though it's decided; revise plainly if wrong.
- **Specific to the detail.** Names, times, counts, addresses. Use the 24-hour clock. No "various," no "several," no "around."
- **Every sentence earns its place.** If a line can be cut without loss, cut it. White space is intentional.
- **Restraint over flourish.** The luxury is in the precision, not the adjectives. Sharp corners, not soft ornament.
- **Never deferential, never showy.** Confidence without display. No flattery, no performance.

> *"Words are tools. Use the sharp ones."*

### The Certainty Filter (run every line through this)

| Test | Question | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Conviction** | Recommending or hedging? | Strike "we could," "perhaps," "if you're open to it." |
| **Specificity** | Is the detail concrete? | Names, times, counts, addresses. No "various" or "several." |
| **Necessity** | Does the sentence earn its place? | If it could be cut without loss, cut it. |

## 3. What the Audience Resonates With

**Who they're talking to:** private principals and their teams — the discreet, ultra-high-net-worth client, the family office, the chief of staff — plus heritage brands and institutions that value restraint. People who are sold to constantly and trust almost none of it.

They respond to:

- **Discretion, assumed.** No name-dropping, no showing off the guest list. The signal of trust is that Iconic *doesn't* talk. Confidentiality is the product.
- **Evident competence.** Not claims of excellence — proof of it, in the specificity and control of every detail. Precision reads as capability.
- **Being in command, not being handled.** The principal keeps the vision; Iconic supplies the route. Never talked down to, never oversold.
- **Absence of friction.** The reassurance that it's all handled — the table held, the sommelier briefed, the timing to the minute — before they had to ask.
- **Restraint as status.** In a loud market, quiet is the flex. The client recognizes the register of people who don't need to shout.
- **Inevitability.** The sense that the work was always going to be this good. Calm certainty, not effort on display.

**Emotional job of the copy:** make a discerning, over-solicited client feel they've finally found the operator who doesn't need managing — and doesn't need to prove it loudly.

## 4. Things Iconic Would Say

Signature phrasing (drawn and extrapolated from the guidelines' register):

- "Tasting at 18:00. Three courses, paired. The chef has held the table; confirm by Friday."
- "We do not help. We facilitate."
- "The client has the vision. We have the route."
- "Every output reads like it was engraved, not generated."
- "Doors at 17:00. Cocktail service in the library."
- "The floor plan is signed. Guest manifest by Wednesday, 12:00."
- "We recommend the smaller room. Thirty-two seated, not sixty."
- "Considered. Quiet. Specific."
- "Confirmed."

**Pattern to reuse:** *[Declarative statement] + [Concrete detail] + [Clear next step, dated.]*
e.g. "The venue is held for the 14th. Deposit confirms it; we need it by Monday."

## 5. Things Iconic Would Never Say

- **Hedges.** "We could probably," "perhaps," "if that works for everyone," "let me know what you think." Iconic states; it does not seek permission.
- **Vague quantities.** "Various options," "several vendors," "a few ideas," "around six." Everything is counted and named.
- **Hospitality-brochure language.** "Unforgettable experiences," "we go above and beyond," "seamless," "bespoke solutions," "world-class." Empty superlatives are the opposite of the brand.
- **Loud, scale-driven claims.** "The biggest," "at scale," "supercharge," anything that trades on volume or velocity. Iconic is quiet and specific by design.
- **Flattery or performance.** "We'd be honored," "what an amazing opportunity," exclamation points, emoji. No showing off, no seeking approval.
- **Name-dropping.** Client names, guest lists, or "we worked with…" Discretion is absolute; the silence is the credential.
- **Padding.** Any sentence that could be cut with no loss. Any decoration that isn't doing work.
- **Options-dumping.** Presenting a menu and asking the client to choose. Iconic recommends the route.

## 6. Sound Bites (ready-to-use, in-voice)

Short, precise lines that match the register — for headers, proposals, and the sales page. Restraint first; every word load-bearing.

**Positioning / headline energy**
- "Engraved, not generated."
- "A standard of iconic."
- "We do not help. We facilitate."
- "Considered. Quiet. Specific."
- "The experience industry got loud. We didn't."

**Authority / trust**
- "Our clients don't put their names on most of what they own. Nor on us."
- "You'll know it was handled. You won't have to ask how."
- "The room is built by people who run rooms."

**Offer / method**
- "You hold the vision. We hold the route."
- "Every detail, decided. Nothing left to chance, nothing left to show off."
- "The table is held. The timing is to the minute. Confirm, and it's done."

**Close energy**
- "One conversation. Then it's handled."
- "Tell us the date. We'll tell you the rest."

*(Use these as templates — keep the rhythm: short clauses, hard stops, concrete detail, no adjectives doing the work of proof.)*

## 7. Writing Rules (quick checklist)

Before any Iconic line ships, check:

1. **Is it declarative?** State the plan; don't ask permission. Cut "could," "perhaps," "if you're open to it."
2. **Is the detail concrete?** A name, a time (24h), a count, an address — never "various" or "around."
3. **Does the sentence earn its place?** If it can be cut without loss, cut it.
4. **Is there a clear next step?** Describe it as decided, with a date, not a question.
5. **Any showing off?** Strip superlatives, flattery, exclamation points, and name-drops.
6. **Does it sound like someone who has done it before?** If it reads like a vendor seeking a booking, rewrite.
7. **Is it quiet?** In doubt, say less. Restraint is the register.

## 8. Voice in One Paragraph (the north star)

> Iconic speaks like the most prepared person in the room — calm, declarative, and exact. It states the plan rather than pitching it, recommends the route rather than offering a menu, and describes the next step rather than asking permission. Every detail is named and timed; nothing is padded, nothing is performed. The client keeps the vision; Iconic quietly supplies everything else, and never needs to say how good it is because the precision already has. Every line sells the same feeling: *it's handled — engraved, not generated — and you never had to raise your voice.*
